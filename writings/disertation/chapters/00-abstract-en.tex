\begin{abstracten}

Condition-based predictive maintenance is a critical strategy in the
transformation of modern manufacturing toward Industry 4.0.
The ability to accurately and timely predict the remaining useful life
(RUL) of critical components enables efficient maintenance interventions,
minimizes unplanned downtime, and enhances operational safety.
Rolling element bearings are the most failure-prone components in
industrial rotating machinery, contributing approximately 40--50\% of
total machine failures.

This dissertation proposes the \emph{Mamba-xLSTM-Net} hybrid architecture
for rolling bearing RUL prediction, which integrates a selective
state-space model (Mamba-3) with matrix-memory Long Short-Term Memory
(mLSTM).
The fusion mechanism is designed based on the non-stationary properties
of bearing vibration signals: Mamba handles long-range temporal
dependencies during gradual degradation phases, while mLSTM preserves
recurrent precision during abrupt changes near failure.

Beyond prediction performance, this dissertation proposes an
interpretability module based on a \emph{Top-$k$ Sparse Autoencoder}
(SAE) applied post-hoc to the latent representations of Mamba-xLSTM-Net.
A quantitative procedure is developed to map SAE features to bearing
characteristic frequencies --- Ball Pass Frequency Outer Race (BPFO),
Ball Pass Frequency Inner Race (BPFI), Ball Spin Frequency (BSF), and
Fundamental Train Frequency (FTF) --- through Pearson correlation against
Hilbert envelope spectrum amplitudes of raw vibration signals.

Empirical validation is conducted on two public run-to-failure datasets:
PHM2012 (FEMTO-PRONOSTIA), comprising 17 NSK 6804 bearings under three
operating conditions, and XJTU-SY, comprising 10 LDK UER204 bearings
(conditions 1 and 2).
A multi-seed protocol with three distinct random initializations
(seeds 42, 43, 44) is applied to ensure statistical rigor.
Performance is evaluated using four simultaneous metrics: RMSE, MAE,
the coefficient of determination R\textsuperscript{2}, and asymmetric
PHM Score.

On PHM2012, \emph{Mamba-xLSTM-Net} achieves RMSE of
$0.242 \pm 0.020$ and demonstrates the highest seed-to-seed stability
among the three compared models.
On XJTU-SY, \emph{Mamba-xLSTM-Net} achieves the highest PHM Score
($0.907$), the metric most relevant to predictive maintenance practice
as it accounts for the asymmetric consequences of prediction errors.
Training curves that continue to improve until epoch 71/75 indicate
that the architecture has not yet reached its full capacity within the
allocated training budget.

For the interpretability pillar, the SAE with 1024 latent dimensions
and $k = 51$ active features ($\approx 5\%$ sparsity) successfully
reconstructs latent representations with MSE loss below $10^{-3}$,
indicating that Mamba-xLSTM-Net latent representations are naturally
sparse.
BPFx mapping reveals that 2.0--2.3\% of SAE features exhibit moderate
correlation ($|r| \geq 0.30$) with envelope spectrum amplitudes near
BPFO and BPFI --- the only characteristic frequencies with hit-rate
$> 0$.
The highest moderate correlations reach $r = 0.507$ (SAE feature
f474~$\leftrightarrow$~BPFI on PHM2012) and $r = 0.501$
(f959~$\leftrightarrow$~BPFO on XJTU-SY), both statistically significant
($n = 300$, $p \ll 0.05$).
These results constitute the first empirical evidence that a deep
learning model trained for RUL prediction learns latent representations
that correspond to classical bearing vibration diagnosis theory.

This research makes three principal contributions:
(1)~the \emph{Mamba-xLSTM-Net} hybrid architecture as a novel approach
combining selective SSM with matrix-memory recurrence for bearing
prognostics;
(2)~an SAE-to-characteristic-frequency mapping procedure as a novel
interpretability methodology for the predictive maintenance domain; and
(3)~empirical evidence of correspondence between deep learning latent
representations and classical vibration physics on two leading benchmark
datasets.

\end{abstracten}
